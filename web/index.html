How this works

A two-way channel between Wellington communities and the Council. You tell WCC what you are seeing; WCC tells you what is happening about it. Everything below is how to use it and what the rules are.

Getting started

There is no account and no password. Open the page and you can read everything public and post straight away. If you want your name on your messages, set it under Message board → Posting as — it is only a label, and you can stay anonymous.

  1. Live map — everything happening right now. Use Near me or type your street to narrow it down.
  2. Report an issue — something is wrong out there. You get a reference code; keep it.
  3. My reports — what happened to what you sent. It updates itself; you never need to ring to ask.
  4. Ask for help — on the Live map, if you or someone near you needs something.

Reporting an issue vs asking for help

These are different and it matters which you use.

  • Report an issue — a condition in the world. Water over a road, a slip, a tree down, no power on a street. It goes on the map for everyone.
  • Ask for help — a person needs something. Getting out, a welfare check, water, medical help. It goes to WCC and responders, and by default only to them.

Neither is an emergency service. If someone is in danger right now, call 111. This is a prototype and nobody is watching it around the clock.

Community guidelines

Short, and all of them are about being useful to a neighbour.

  1. Say what you saw, where. "Water over the road on Hutt Rd near the onramp, ankle deep" is actionable. "FLOODING!!!" is not, and the form will ask you for more.
  2. Do not report what you have not seen. Passing on a rumour as a sighting sends a crew to the wrong street.
  3. Do not post other people's details. If a message is about an identifiable person — an address, a health condition, a name — mark it Only officials.
  4. One message per thing. Repeating yourself does not make it more urgent; it makes the board harder to read. Several people reporting the same thing is fine and useful — that is corroboration, and it stacks.
  5. Keep it civil. Complaints about the Council are legitimate, but an emergency board during an event is not where they help anyone.
  6. Do not impersonate anyone. Officials are badged with a verified access card. Claiming to be one is the one thing here that could get somebody hurt.

How moderation works

Photos, offers of help and live feeds from unverified residents wait for a moderator before the public sees them. That is because this map looks like it comes from the Council, and a wrong address for "water here" sends people to the wrong street.

Nothing is ever deleted. When a moderator withholds a message, it leaves the public feed and a visible marker stays where it was — so a gap in a conversation is always accounted for, and moderation can never look like silent censorship. The original and the reason stay visible to officials.

Moderators are either card holders, or people the board has noticed are consistently useful. That second route is scored on countable things — reports the Council acted on, substantive posts, time active — and any message of yours that gets flagged rules you out.

Your privacy

  • Your location stays in your browser. The Near me filter and the address lookup run on your device against data shipped with the page. Nothing about where you are is sent to us.
  • Photos are stripped. If a photo carries GPS we use it to place the pin, show you what we read, and then remove all metadata from the copy we publish.
  • Contact details are never public. A phone number on an offer or a request goes to officials only, even when the rest of the item is public.
  • Requests for help default to private. You can choose to share one with neighbours — often the fastest responder is two streets away — but you have to choose it.

Access cards

Officials sign in with a code printed on a card they carry. No email, no text message, no login service — during an emergency those are exactly what fails. Type the code, or tap one in the sign-in dialog.

A card only ever grants cards below its own level, so handing one out can never create an equal. Lost a card? Tell whoever issued it and it is cancelled immediately, along with any session opened using it.

Common questions

I reported something. How do I find out what happened?
Open My reports. It updates on its own the moment a staff member changes the status — you do not need to refresh, and you do not need to ring the call centre.
I lost my reference code.
It is saved on the device you reported from. From another device, paste it into My reports → Have a code from another device. If it is genuinely gone, report the issue again — duplicates are not a problem here, they stack as corroboration.
Why was my message rejected?
Almost always because it was too short to act on. The form tells you exactly what is missing. It is not a judgement about you — the same rule applies to Council staff.
Someone posted something wrong. What do I do?
A moderator can withhold it. Post on the same board saying what you know instead — a correction from a neighbour who was there is usually faster and more trusted than moderation.
Can I see what is happening on just my street?
Yes. Near me, or type your street, then pick a radius from 1 to 20 km. The map draws the radius so you can see what you are excluding.
Does this work if the internet is down?
Partly, and deliberately. The map and the street index are stored in the page, so it keeps working on a local network with no connection to the outside world. Access cards work the same way. Sending a report still needs to reach the server.
Is this an official Council service?
No. It is a prototype built in one day at Impact Lab Wellington. In an emergency, call 111.

    Issued cards

    Earned trust

    Who the board thinks is worth moderation, scored on countable behaviour. Any message of theirs that has been flagged rules them out entirely. Automation can grant a community moderator card and nothing higher — a moderator cannot issue cards, so the bot can never manufacture an official.
